Partnership

What We Look for
in a Founder

← Back to Insights
Partnership January 2026 5 min read

When we meet a founder, we are not primarily evaluating a business plan. We are evaluating a person. Markets change, plans change, competition arrives from unexpected directions — but the character of the individual at the centre of an enterprise rarely does.

This is not to say that business fundamentals do not matter. Of course they do. The market opportunity, the competitive moat, the unit economics, the team below the founder — all of these are essential inputs to any investment decision. But in our experience, the single most predictive factor in whether a private company succeeds over a decade is not the market or the model. It is the person leading it.

When we partner with a founder, we are entering a relationship that could last ten years or more. We will sit alongside them through periods of growth and difficulty, through disagreements about strategy and priorities, through the inevitable moments when the plan meets reality and requires revision. What we need to know, before any of that begins, is: can we trust this person? Will they do what they say? Can they handle adversity? Do they care about building something real, or merely about an exit?

Integrity before everything

The first thing we look for is integrity. Not ethics as a policy, but ethics as a default — the instinct to do the right thing even when no one is watching, even when the easier path is available. In our years of working with businesses, we have seen more value destroyed by character failures than by strategic mistakes. Strategic mistakes can be corrected. Character failures compound.

Integrity reveals itself in small things. How does a founder talk about competitors — with honest respect or dismissive contempt? When asked a question they do not know the answer to, do they say so or construct a plausible-sounding response? How do they treat junior members of their team? These are not trivial questions. They are signals of the culture a founder is building, and culture is the most durable competitive advantage a business can possess.

"The first thing we look for is integrity — not ethics as a policy, but ethics as a default. The instinct to do the right thing even when no one is watching."

Conviction, but not certainty

We look for founders who have deep, earned conviction in what they are building — not because someone told them the market was large, but because they have spent years studying the problem, understanding the customer, and testing their assumptions against reality. That kind of conviction sustains you through difficult periods. It is what makes a founder get up after a significant setback and continue building.

But we are wary of founders who mistake conviction for certainty. The best founders we have worked with hold strong views loosely. They have the intellectual honesty to revise their model when the evidence demands it, the humility to recruit people who are better than them in specific areas, and the curiosity to keep learning even when they are the most experienced person in the room. Certainty closes the mind. Conviction keeps it open.

Building for the long term

We are a long-term investor. We do not structure our partnerships around exit horizons or target multiples. We invest in businesses we intend to support for a decade or more, and we look for founders who share that orientation. The question we ask ourselves — and sometimes ask directly — is: is this person building a business, or building for a sale?

The answer shapes everything. Founders building for the long term make different decisions — about people, about culture, about capital allocation, about which partnerships to pursue and which to decline. They are more willing to forego short-term profit for long-term competitive position. They invest in their workforce. They maintain standards even when corners could be cut. They care about what their business will be in ten years, not just what it looks like at the next funding round.

These are the founders we want to back. Not because they produce the fastest returns, but because they produce the most enduring ones. And at Adera Holdings, enduring is what we are here for.

If you are building something you believe in — something you intend to grow with care, with integrity, and with a genuine long-term vision — we would very much like to hear from you.
