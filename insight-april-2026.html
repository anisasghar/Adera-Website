Investment Philosophy

Conviction Over Consensus:
Investing Through Uncertainty

← Back to Insights
Investment Philosophy April 2026 5 min read

Uncertainty has become the defining feature of global markets. Trade policy shifts overnight, geopolitical alliances realign, and capital flows respond with the kind of volatility that erodes confidence and shortens horizons. In this environment, the instinct for many investors is to wait. We believe the opposite is true.

Periods of genuine macro uncertainty are not obstacles to long-term investing — they are its most productive moments. When consensus fractures and short-term participants step back, the pricing of quality assets begins to reflect fear rather than fundamentals. For investors with permanent capital, clear conviction, and the discipline to act against the prevailing mood, this is precisely the environment that creates lasting value.

This is not contrarianism for its own sake. It is the logical consequence of a structural advantage that patient capital holds over reactive capital. When others are constrained by redemption pressure, mark-to-market reporting, or simply the paralysis of uncertainty, a firm like Adera Holdings is free to do what it does best: evaluate businesses on their intrinsic merit and act with the kind of long-term conviction that short-horizon participants cannot sustain.

The cost of waiting for clarity

The appeal of waiting for clarity is understandable. No one wants to commit capital into a deteriorating macro environment. But the clarity investors wait for rarely arrives cleanly — and by the time it does, the opportunity it would have created is already gone. Markets do not wait for consensus. They reprice before the narrative resolves.

History is consistent on this point. The most consequential entry points for long-term capital — across private equity, infrastructure, and direct investment — have almost always coincided with periods of elevated uncertainty. The businesses that were acquired, the partnerships that were forged, and the platforms that were built during those windows went on to generate returns that calmer periods simply could not replicate.

"The time to buy is when there's blood in the streets — even if the blood is your own." — Baron Rothschild

Conviction is not the absence of doubt

What separates conviction from recklessness is not certainty — it is process. A well-constructed investment thesis accounts for the range of macro outcomes rather than assuming a single one. It stress-tests the business model against adverse scenarios, evaluates the quality of management under pressure, and assesses whether the structural drivers of value creation remain intact regardless of how the near-term noise resolves.

At Adera Holdings, our investment process has always been built around this discipline. We do not attempt to forecast macro outcomes with precision — no one can. What we do is identify businesses with the resilience to perform across a range of environments, the management teams with the judgement to navigate uncertainty, and the structural positions within their sectors that compound value independent of the economic cycle.

Where we are looking now

The current environment has created particular interest for us in three areas. First, infrastructure assets where long-term contracted cash flows provide genuine insulation from macro volatility and where repricing has created entry points that were unavailable twelve months ago. Second, technology-enabled businesses in sectors that remain structurally underpenetrated — where near-term earnings pressure obscures multi-year growth potential that patient capital is uniquely positioned to capture. Third, direct acquisition opportunities in the mid-market, where owners facing uncertainty are increasingly willing to engage with partners who can offer both capital and genuine operational depth.

In each case, our approach is the same: assess the business on its fundamentals, not its current environment. Uncertainty compresses valuations. Conviction, applied with rigour and patience, converts that compression into long-term value.

We have always believed that the most important quality in an investment partner is not the ability to predict what happens next — it is the discipline to remain rational when others cannot. That discipline is easier to articulate than to practise. But it is the foundation on which enduring returns are built, and it is the principle that continues to guide everything we do at Adera Holdings.
