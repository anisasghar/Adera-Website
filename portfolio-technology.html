Technology

Technology is not a sector we approach as a theme or a trend. It is a domain of enduring structural importance — one in which the quality of founders matters more than the quality of the macro backdrop, and where patient, high-conviction backing of the right businesses can generate returns that shorter-horizon capital consistently fails to capture.

Our technology portfolio is focused on growth-stage businesses that have moved beyond the early product risk and are building toward sustained market leadership. We invest across artificial intelligence and machine learning, enterprise software, deep technology, and technology-enabled financial services. What these investments share is not a common subsector but a common character: founder-led businesses with genuine intellectual differentiation, defensible positions, and leadership teams that are building for the long term.

What we look for in founders

We have found, over many investments, that the single most important variable in technology outcomes is the quality of the founder. Not the market size, not the technology stack, not the go-to-market model — though all of these matter — but the person at the centre of the organisation who sets the culture, makes the critical decisions under uncertainty, and determines whether the business compounds or stalls.

We back founders who demonstrate genuine domain expertise, intellectual honesty about the challenges they face, and an ability to attract and retain exceptional people. We are drawn to those who have thought carefully about the long-term architecture of their business — not simply the next fundraising round — and who are building organisations capable of sustaining competitive advantage as markets evolve. We do not expect founders to have all the answers. We do expect them to ask the right questions.

"We are not passive investors in technology businesses. We take a seat at the table, bring our network to bear, and commit to the kind of long-term partnership that the best founders deserve and the best outcomes require."

Our investment approach

We participate at the board level in our technology investments, acting as genuine partners to management rather than monitors of financial performance. This means contributing to hiring decisions, strategic planning, partnership development, and — where appropriate — introductions to customers, regulators, and co-investors who can accelerate the business. Our network across financial services, real estate, infrastructure, and government gives us access and relationships that are often directly relevant to the enterprise and B2B businesses we back.

We do not operate to an exit horizon. We hold technology positions for as long as we believe the business is compounding value and the partnership remains productive. In practice, this means we have held certain technology investments through multiple market cycles, resisting the pressure to realise gains prematurely when our conviction in the underlying business remains intact. This approach is not universally applicable — some businesses require exits to fund the next stage of growth, and we support those decisions when they serve the company's long-term interests — but our default disposition is to remain invested and to let time work in our favour.

Our areas of particular focus include AI and machine learning infrastructure, enterprise software for regulated industries, deep technology with applications in materials science and biotechnology, and fintech platforms that are genuinely improving access to or the quality of financial services. In each case, we are looking for businesses where the technology creates a durable and widening moat — not simply a first-mover advantage that can be replicated with sufficient capital.
